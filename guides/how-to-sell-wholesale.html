How to sell wholesale to shops

Shops buy at wholesale and sell at retail. Get four things ready before you contact one: prices that still pay you at wholesale, a line sheet, simple terms and an easy way to take orders.

1. Wholesale prices that still pay you

Work out what one item really costs you, including your time, then use the common rule wholesale = 2 × cost to make and retail = 2 × wholesale. A candle that costs $7.51 to make sells to shops for $15.02 and in their shop for $30.04, so the shop keeps half. If the wholesale price leaves you too little, raise the retail price rather than squeezing the shop: they need that margin to stock you. (How the $7.51 was worked out.)

2. A line sheet

One page per product line: photo, product name and code (SKU), short description, wholesale price, suggested retail price, and the case pack (sold in 6s, 12s...). Add your business name, contact and terms at the top.

3. Simple terms

  • Minimum first order: often $100-$250, so each order is worth packing.
  • Case packs: sell in multiples (6 candles, 12 packs of melts) to keep packing simple.
  • Shipping: free over an amount (for example $300), a flat charge below it.
  • Payment: paid before shipping for a first order is common.

The shop pays $477.52 for goods it can sell for $955.04: a 50% margin for them. You earn your full wholesale price on 44 items in one shipment, instead of 44 separate sales and fees.

Check what your selling platform allows: some marketplaces have their own wholesale programs, and selling to a shop directly means you invoice and take payment yourself.
